# Pitch script, 240 seconds

Timed for local judging. Read the SAY lines aloud. Do the SCREEN cue at each mark.
Pace is calm, about 140 words per minute. SAY text is about 510 words, so the read runs about 3 minutes 40 seconds and leaves room for clicks.

## 01 who, first 45 seconds

SCREEN: Cold open on the intro. Two ground strips, marks planted on them. Do not touch anything for 3 full seconds. Then slow drag to orbit.

SAY:
> "Everything on this screen is still out there.
> Eighteen pieces of hardware we left on the Moon and Mars, eight on the Moon and ten on Mars, and each one is still sitting where we left it.
> I am [name], and I built a curator workspace called Abandoned but Not Forgotten. It keeps what each object is, why it was left, and what it taught. The work started because the hardware is fine and the paperwork around it keeps degrading."

## 02 why, 0:45 to 1:45

SCREEN: Hold on the viewport key. Colour is certainty, shape is kind. Then open Schiaparelli EDM lander and hold on the status note.

SAY:
> "The people who know why each object was left are retiring. Coordinates get quoted with a precision nobody actually has. When the next mission lands nearby, nobody owns the question of what should happen to the old hardware.
> Here is one record that shows the cost. Schiaparelli crashed on Mars in 2016. The app writes that an impact and debris field is expected near the intended landing site, with PIA21131 from the Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter as the evidence. The HiRISE image is P I A 2 1 1 3 1. A planner who copies an invented coordinate inherits the error. A planner who reads expected near the site can work around it."

## 03 what, 1:45 to 2:45

SCREEN: Click Surveyor 1. Linger on the badges, Well documented and Intact and 1966. Scroll through What it is into Why it was abandoned. Point at the NASA card for PIA12910, with NASA own caption. The photo id is P I A 1 2 9 1 0.

SAY:
> "The app is a single column mission flow. The top has views for Moon, Mars, and the full story. The ledger holds everything and searches down into narratives. The dossier holds sources, who cares, and what should happen next.
> Surveyor 1 is the healthy case. It finished its work in 1966 and recovery was never planned. Leaving it was part of the plan. That distinction is in the record itself.
> Every claim points at evidence. The Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter photo of Surveyor 1 ships inside the app and works offline. Film sits under the story. The story comes first. For Luna 2 there is no landing footage, so the record links a Goddard visualization of the Moon and labels it as context."

## 04 how, 2:45 to 4:00

SCREEN: Type collision. It lands on Luna 2 through its lesson. Filter to Mars and show the rail note that keeps the open record. Then file a next action on Surveyor 1, quit the app, reopen it, show the action is still there.

SAY:
> "Search reaches into meanings. The word collision finds Luna 2 through its lesson. Filtering keeps your place. The record you were reading stays open and the rail tells you it sits outside the filter.
> This is a work queue curators write in. I will type a real next action now. Two landing site studies quote different radii for the same hardware. It saves to a SQLite file at your own archive path. I will quit and reopen. It is still there.
> The build is verified end to end with 404 automated checks, and the packaged folder runs with no install and no network. The next step is to wire the record shape to public archives for orbiter imagery and heritage registries, so a group of curators can write to a shared record."
